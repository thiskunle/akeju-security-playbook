## What counts as an incident

Examples: a stolen password, a lost laptop, ransomware, a payment sent to a fraudster, customer data sent to the wrong person, or an unexplained login.

Staff report anything suspicious to [contact] right away. Nobody is punished for reporting in good faith, including reporting their own mistake.

## Steps

### 1. Identify

- Write down what happened, when it was noticed, and who noticed it.
- Open an incident log and record every action with the time.
- Rate it. **High:** customer data, money or operations affected. **Medium:** one account or device affected. **Low:** an attempt that failed.

### 2. Contain

- Disconnect affected devices from the network. **Do not turn them off or wipe them**, because that can destroy evidence.
- Change passwords and sign out all sessions on affected accounts.
- Check affected mailboxes for forwarding rules an attacker may have added.
- Call your bank right away if money was sent.
- Call your insurer before hiring outside help, since many policies require it.

### 3. Remove and recover

- Find how the attacker got in and close that path.
- Rebuild affected systems from clean sources.
- Restore data from a backup made before the incident.
- Watch the affected accounts closely for 30 days.

### 4. Notify

Decide with your privacy contact. Under PIPEDA, Canadian organizations must:

- Report a breach of personal information to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner when it creates a **real risk of significant harm**.
- Notify the affected individuals in that same case, as soon as feasible.
- Keep a record of **every** breach of security safeguards for 24 months, even those that do not need reporting.

Also check your customer contracts, which may set shorter deadlines. Report crime to local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

### 5. Learn

Within two weeks, meet and answer: what happened, what worked, what did not, and what changes now. Give each change an owner and a date.

## Practise

Run a 30-minute tabletop exercise at least once a year. Example: "The bookkeeper paid a fake invoice for $18,000 this morning. What do we do first?"
